# Social Carousel

A carousel earns two things: a swipe, then a save. The hook buys the swipe. Delivering
exactly what the hook promised buys the save.

## The rule that governs everything else

**If the hook promises a number, every one of them is on the slides.** A carousel that
says "7 prompts" and then asks people to comment for the prompts is bait, and it
trains the audience to ignore the next hook. The offer comes *after* the payoff, never
instead of it.

## What you need first

The topic, the audience, the platform, and — if there is one — the specific thing the
user wants people to do at the end.

## Steps

1. **Find the countable promise.** What is the number? Seven prompts, five mistakes,
   three settings. "Some tips" is not a promise and gives nobody a reason to swipe.

2. **Write the hook slide.** One pattern interrupt, then the promise. Short lines,
   hard returns, no paragraph. Lead with the loss before the fix — *"You're paying
   $240 a year for something free"* lands harder than *"here are some free
   alternatives"*. Write five hooks and pick, do not write one.

3. **One idea per slide.** If a slide needs a comma-splice to fit, it is two slides.
   Interior slides are read in about a second and a half.

4. **Deliver the count.** Number the items visibly. The number is the contract.

5. **Add one "why it matters" slide** near the end — the mechanism, the reason this
   works. This is the slide that gets screenshotted.

6. **Close on the ask.** *Save this* is usually the right ask, because saves predict
   both reach and purchase. Follow is a footnote, not the headline.

7. **Write the caption as one line.** The slides carry the payload. A long caption on
   a carousel is a second, worse version of the post.

## Output format

Slide by slide, numbered, with the exact text per slide and a note on any slide meant
to carry emphasis. Then the one-line caption. Then two alternative hook slides.

Target 7–10 slides. Past twelve, completion rate falls off a cliff.

## Rules

- **No invented numbers.** No view counts, no income figures, no "I grew to 50k" the
  user has not done. Tool and mechanism claims need no personal result and are
  available immediately.
- No claim that a paid tool is free. If only part is free, name the part.
- Second person, present tense, short sentences.

## Where this breaks

- **Text-heavy slides fail on mobile.** If a slide will not read at thumbnail size, it
  is not a slide, it is a paragraph.
- **A carousel cannot rescue a topic nobody cares about.** Format amplifies interest;
  it does not create it.
- **On LinkedIn the first two lines of the caption matter more than the hook slide**,
  because the preview is text. The one-line caption rule is Instagram-shaped — on
  LinkedIn, front-load the caption instead.
