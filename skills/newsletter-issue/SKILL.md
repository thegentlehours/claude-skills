# Newsletter Issue

A newsletter is judged on the *next* open, not this one. So the job is not to
maximise clicks today — it is to leave the reader glad they opened, so the next
subject line gets the benefit of the doubt.

## What you need first

1. The topic, or the raw material
2. Who is on the list, and how they got there — people who joined for a free tools
   list want tools, not life advice
3. What the user is selling right now, if anything
4. Roughly how often they send, and when they last sent

If it has been months, that changes the opening: acknowledge it in one line and move
on. Do not pretend continuity that does not exist.

## Steps

1. **One idea.** Say it in a sentence before writing anything. If the issue needs two
   sentences, it is two issues — say so and offer to split it.

2. **Open with the thing itself.** No "hope you've had a great week", no preamble
   about what the email will cover. The first line is the first beat of the idea.
   Cut whatever you wrote first; it is almost always throat-clearing.

3. **Give the useful part early.** If the reader stops a third of the way in, they
   should already have got something. Structure for abandonment — most readers do
   not finish, and that is fine if the top carried the value.

4. **Be specific about what you actually did.** A newsletter's advantage over a blog
   post is that it can say "I tried this on Tuesday and it broke". Use it.

5. **One ask.** Reply, click, buy, or nothing. Never two. If there is a genuine offer,
   put it at the end and mark it clearly as an offer rather than blending it in.

6. **Subject line, last.** Write five. Pick the specific one over the clever one.
   Then check it against what the email actually delivers — a subject that overpromises
   costs the next open, which is the only thing that matters.

7. **Preview text** as a second line that adds to the subject rather than repeating it.

## Output format

Subject (plus four alternatives), preview text, then the full email body ready to
send. Then one line naming who this issue will bore, so the user can judge the
trade.

Length: whatever the idea needs. Most issues are too long by a third — cut before
sending.

## Rules

- No fake personal anecdotes. If the user did not do the thing, do not write that they
  did.
- No engagement bait — no "hit reply and tell me" unless the user will actually read
  and answer the replies.
- No invented results, subscriber counts or testimonials.
- Match the user's voice from samples if provided.

## Where this breaks

- **It cannot know what the list already received.** Repeating an idea from three
  weeks ago is invisible to this skill and obvious to a subscriber.
- **Deliverability is not writing.** If opens are collapsing, the cause is more often
  authentication, list hygiene or send reputation than the words. Check SPF, DKIM and
  DMARC before rewriting anything.
- A good issue cannot fix a list built from the wrong lead magnet. People who came for
  a free tool list are not automatically buyers of a course.
